は (wa) vs が (ga): think of it as a spotlight

KKaz · Published Sep 30, 2026 · Updated Oct 1, 2026 · 8 min read

In 30 seconds

  • は names the topic — what the sentence is about.
  • が picks out who or what — often new information, or the answer to “who?”
  • Question words like 誰だれ (who) take が, never は.

Why this pair is so confusing

Textbooks often translate both は and が as nothing at all — there’s no English word for either. So you end up guessing. If you’ve ever stared at a sentence and flipped a coin between the two, you’re in good company.

Here is the short version: は tells the listener what you’re talking about. が tells them which one.

Think of it like this

Picture a stage. は is the stage light that’s already on — “OK, we’re talking about me now.” が is a spotlight that swings onto one person in the crowd — “It’s Tanaka who came, not anyone else.”

This is just an analogy to help it stick — not a rule that covers every case.

は: setting the topic

Use は when you introduce what the sentence is about. In a self-introduction, you are the topic.

EXAMPLE私わたしは学生がくせいです。Watashi wa gakusei desu.I’m a student. (As for me — I’m a student.)

が: pointing at “which one”

When someone asks “who?”, both the question and the answer use が. The answer is new information, so it gets the spotlight.

QUESTION誰だれが来きましたか。Dare ga kimashita ka?Who came?
ANSWER田中たなかさんが来きました。Tanaka-san ga kimashita.Tanaka came.

Common mistakes

✕誰だれは来きましたか。 A question word can’t be the topic, because the topic is something you already know. Use が.
○誰だれが来きましたか。Dare ga kimashita ka? — “Who came?”
△（自己紹介じこしょうかいで）私わたしが学生がくせいです。 Not wrong, but odd in a self-introduction: it sounds like “I’m the one who’s the student” — as if answering “Which of you is the student?”

Try it yourself

Next time you write a Japanese sentence, ask: “Am I naming the topic, or answering which one?” Then write one sentence with は and one with が in the comments — we’ll take a look.
