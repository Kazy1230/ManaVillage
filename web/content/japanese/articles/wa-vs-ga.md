Textbooks often translate both <b>は</b> and <b>が</b> as nothing at all — there’s no English word for either. So when you study **wa vs ga**, you end up guessing. If you’ve ever flipped a coin between the two, you’re in good company.

<div class="points"><span class="lbl">IN 30 SECONDS</span>

- <b>は</b> names the **topic** — what the sentence is about.
- <b>が</b> picks out **who or what** — often new information, or the answer to “who?”
- Question words like {誰|だれ} (who) take <b>が</b>, never <b>は</b>.

</div>

![A stage with a striped kabuki curtain. A spotlight shines on one stick figure, labeled が = this one!, while two gray figures stand on the stage labeled は = the stage.](/illustrations/wa-vs-ga/core.webp)

## Think of a stage and a spotlight

Here is the short version: ==は tells the listener what you’re talking about. が tells them which one.==

<div class="analogy"><span class="lbl">THINK OF IT LIKE THIS</span><p>Picture a stage. <b>は</b> is the stage light that’s already on — “OK, we’re talking about <em>me</em> now.” <b>が</b> is a spotlight that swings onto one person in the crowd — “It’s <em>Tanaka</em> who came, not anyone else.” This is just a picture to help it stick, not a rule that covers every case.</p></div>

## は: setting the topic

Use <b>は</b> when you introduce what the sentence is about. In a self-introduction, you are the topic.

<div class="example"><span class="lbl">EXAMPLE</span><span class="jpx">{私|わたし}<mark>は</mark>{学生|がくせい}です。</span><span class="ro">Watashi wa gakusei desu.</span><span class="tr">I’m a student. (As for me — I’m a student.)</span></div>

## が: pointing at “which one”

When someone asks “who?”, both the question and the answer use <b>が</b>. The answer is new information, so it gets the spotlight.

<div class="ex-pair">
<div class="example"><span class="lbl">QUESTION</span><span class="jpx">{誰|だれ}<mark>が</mark>{来|き}ましたか。</span><span class="ro">Dare ga kimashita ka?</span><span class="tr">Who came?</span></div>
<div class="example"><span class="lbl">ANSWER</span><span class="jpx">{田中|たなか}さん<mark>が</mark>{来|き}ました。</span><span class="ro">Tanaka-san ga kimashita.</span><span class="tr">Tanaka came.</span></div>
</div>

## The mistake almost everyone makes

A question word can’t be the topic, because the topic is something you already know.

<div class="ex-pair">
<div class="example ng"><span class="lbl">NG</span><span class="jpx">{誰|だれ}は{来|き}ましたか。</span><span class="ro">Dare wa kimashita ka?</span><span class="tr">(intended: Who came?)</span><span class="why">{誰|だれ} asks for something unknown, so it can’t be marked with は.</span></div>
<div class="example ok"><span class="lbl">OK</span><span class="jpx">{誰|だれ}<mark>が</mark>{来|き}ましたか。</span><span class="ro">Dare ga kimashita ka?</span><span class="tr">Who came?</span></div>
</div>

And one that isn’t wrong, but sounds odd: saying {私|わたし}が{学生|がくせい}です in a self-introduction sounds like “*I’m* the one who’s the student” — as if you were answering “Which of you is the student?”

### Comparing look-alike words

Synonym guides use a table like this one.

| | {見|み}る (miru) | {見|み}える (mieru) | {眺|なが}める (nagameru) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meaning | look, watch | be visible, can see | gaze at, look out over |
| On purpose? | Yes | No — it comes into view | Yes — slowly, for a while |
| Particle | 〜を{見|み}る | 〜が{見|み}える | 〜を{眺|なが}める |

Next time you write a Japanese sentence, ask: “Am I naming the topic, or answering *which one*?” Then post one sentence with <b>は</b> and one with <b>が</b> in the comments.
